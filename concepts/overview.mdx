Google is built around a clear, layered structure that keeps your team's work organized at every level. Whether you're managing a single project or coordinating dozens of teams across your organization, every piece of work lives inside a well-defined hierarchy — from the top-level workspace all the way down to individual tasks, the people assigned to them, and the tools connected to your workflow.

## Core Concepts

The five building blocks below form the foundation of everything you do in Google. Understanding how they relate to each other will help you set up your workspace effectively and get your team moving quickly.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Workspaces" icon="building">
    A workspace is the top-level container for your entire organization. All projects, members, settings, and billing live inside a single workspace. Every team member you invite belongs to the workspace before they join any individual project.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Projects" icon="folder-open">
    Projects are organized units of work within your workspace. Each project has its own task list, settings, members, and status. Use projects to group related work — by team, product area, client, or initiative.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tasks" icon="circle-check">
    Tasks are the individual action items inside a project. Each task has a title, status, assignee, due date, and optional attachments or comments. Tasks are the primary unit of work your team creates, updates, and completes every day.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Members" icon="users">
    Members are the users in your workspace. Each member is assigned a role — Owner, Admin, Member, or Viewer — that controls what they can see and do. You can also assign members to specific projects with project-level permissions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Integrations" icon="plug">
    Integrations connect Google to the external tools your team already uses — such as Slack, GitHub, Jira, and more. Once connected, integrations let you sync data, trigger automations, and surface information without switching between apps.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Automations" icon="bolt">
    Automations let you define rules that run automatically in response to events in your workspace. Use them to assign tasks, send notifications, update statuses, or trigger actions in connected integrations — without manual intervention.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How These Concepts Relate

Every object in Google belongs to a parent. A workspace contains projects. Projects contain tasks. Tasks belong to members. This hierarchy is intentional — it means permissions, settings, and visibility all flow downward in a predictable way.

When you invite a member to your workspace, they gain access according to their workspace-level role. You can then add them to specific projects, where they may have a different, more granular role. Tasks within those projects can be assigned to any project member, creating a clear chain of ownership from the organization level all the way down to a single to-do item.

Integrations sit alongside this hierarchy rather than inside it. A connected tool like Slack operates at the workspace level, but you can configure it to send notifications scoped to a specific project or even a specific task status. This gives you flexibility without sacrificing the clarity of the core structure.

<Note>
  Every workspace starts with a single Owner — the person who created it. The Owner role cannot be deleted and can only be transferred to another Admin. See [Roles and Permissions](/concepts/permissions) for a full breakdown.
</Note>
